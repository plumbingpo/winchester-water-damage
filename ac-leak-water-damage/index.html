AC Leak Water Damage in Winchester: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Ceilings Under the Unit

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Mold growth, if any, and the containment and removal it calls for.
  • How many days the condensate dripped before the system was shut off.
  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.
  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.
