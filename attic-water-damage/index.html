Attic Water Damage in Winchester

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Winchester inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Water droplets or rust on the tips of nails that poke through the roof sheathing
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Flattened, matted or discolored insulation that stands out from what surrounds it

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Air Conditioner Drips

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Wet Wiring

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.
