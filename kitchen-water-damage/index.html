Kitchen Water Damage: What Winchester Homeowners Should Know

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. For homeowners in Winchester, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

What It Looks Like

  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Drips at the Sink Drain

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

The Ice Maker Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. The appliance hides all of it until the floor changes.
