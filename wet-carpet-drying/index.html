Wet Carpet Drying Help in Winchester

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In Winchester, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Warning Signs

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Subfloor Damage

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Mold Under the Carpet

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.
