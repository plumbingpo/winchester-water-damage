Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Winchester Restoration know it well. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Our Structural Drying Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Winchester Restoration:

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
