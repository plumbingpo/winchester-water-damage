Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Winchester

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Winchester crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • Washer supply hoses made of rubber that get weak over the years and burst from the pressure.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
  • An overflow caused by an overloaded drum or by using too much detergent.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring and Subfloor

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Cabinet Damage

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Mold Behind Appliances

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.
