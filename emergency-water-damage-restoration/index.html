Maintenance Tips for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.
  • Save an emergency restoration company's number in your contacts ahead of time.
  • Know where your electrical panel is so power to wet rooms can be cut safely.
  • Any trip beyond a few days is a reason to turn the main supply off before you go.
  • Pipes running through unheated areas need insulation to keep them from freezing and bursting.
  • Sinks, dishwashers, washing machines and water heaters are all good spots for a leak sensor.

What a Professional Brings to Emergency Water Damage Restoration

In an emergency, the hard part is not mopping up what you can see. The difficulty lies in tracking where the remaining water travelled. Water runs under baseboards, into wall cavities and beneath flooring within minutes. Our team uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find it, so the wet areas you cannot see are treated the same night instead of being found weeks later as a smell or a stain.

A night crew also brings equipment no household has on hand: truck-mounted extractors, commercial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the space. The crew is trained to stay safe near contaminated water and wet wiring, and from hour one it records the loss in photos and moisture readings. Those records are useful if you file an insurance claim. You get one team that handles the emergency and stays with the job until the home is dry.

Signs It Is Time for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Part of your ceiling bows downward because water is pooling on top of it.
  • The breaker box, a running appliance or cords along the floor sit in the path of the water.
  • A water heater tank has failed and is emptying across the floor.
  • Water is still flowing and you cannot find or reach the shutoff valve.
  • Walls on the lower floor are wet from top to bottom because of a leak overhead.
  • Wet carpet, wood flooring or wallboard has stayed saturated longer than a few hours.
  • The house was flooded when you walked in, with no telling how many hours it had been going.
